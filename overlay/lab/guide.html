SMILE LAB · BROADCAST KIT · ROUND 4

Wiring captures, alerts & sponsors into OBS

Everything in the lab runs on one honest principle: the HTML overlay is chrome, OBS owns the sources. A browser source is a sandboxed web page — it cannot reach into your camera or window captures. So every scene renders card frames with transparent holes at exact, documented coordinates, and your captures sit underneath, showing through. This guide gives you every coordinate, preset and switch. All numbers assume a 1920×1080 base canvas (the scenes letterbox-scale themselves to any other resolution — and every type size is tuned so the stream stays legible even when viewers watch on tiny phone screens).

02Quick start (per scene, ~3 minutes)

Add the browser source with the scene URL (see lab index) · Width 1920 · Height 1080 · tick “Shutdown source when not visible” OFF, “Refresh browser when scene becomes active” ON.
Open ?cal=1 (append to the URL) — the scene draws dashed outlines on every hole and prints a live slot sheet with exact X · Y · W · H values.
Add your captures below the overlay. For each hole: right-click source → Transform → Edit Transform → set Position X/Y and Size W/H from the slot sheet.
Ratio mismatch? Hold Alt (Opt on macOS) and drag the red crop handles — crop the capture to the hole's aspect. The hole is transparent, so anything outside the slot never shows.
Save the scene as a preset (“SMILE · STUDIO 2-CAM”, “SMILE · CINEMA PURE”…). Switching layouts = switching OBS scenes; the overlay needs no touching. Remove ?cal=1 when done.

04Cams & guests — no more phone-strip webcams

R3's tall person-deck made a lone 16:9 webcam read like a phone video. Round 4 fixes it two ways:

STUDIO R4 — the 3-module rail

One side column holds exactly three things: Camera · AD · Chat. The camera is a full 16:9 of the rail (400×225) with its nameplate; the AD keeps whatever aspect its video actually is (16:9, 3:1 banner, 1:1, 9:16 — cycle with D) and chat absorbs the rest. Priority is protected: cam and chat never shrink for the ad. S swaps the rail to the other side.

PANEL — round-table format

For 3–5 people: guests as 16:9 cards across the top (2–5 via keys or ?g=N), display capture + chat below. An auto-rotating gold spotlight marks the active speaker with an “ON THE DESK” banner (E to force). Nobody's cam is ever a weird ratio.

05Alert engine v2 — normal toasts, honest limits

Built on your feedback: no more ×N merge counters — the same person repeating inside 9 seconds is silently dropped, like a moderation rule. What renders is a plain toast stack:

What it does now

Toasts stack like normal toasts — newest enters at the bottom (closest to the ticker), older ones sit above.

The zone has a max height (42% of the screen, URL-tunable). When the stack grows past it, the oldest toast leaves first — the content can never be buried.

Max 3 live by default (?amax), queue capped at 10; follows drop before tips; trades/lessons/news jump the queue.

Restyled pill cards: quiet dark glass, rounded 20, gold type chip, hairline progress — no loud colour bars.

Placement A/B + types

Z cycles the zone left → centre → right, always just above the ticker. STUDIO R4 defaults LEFT, DESK defaults CENTRE — run both and keep the winner.

New event types: NEXT LESSON · CLOSE TRADE (green/red by side) · MARKET FLASH alongside tips, subs, follows, raids, questions — each with its multiplatform badge.

Tune: ?amax=3 ?adur=8 ?amaxh=42 ?ademo=1 · Keys: A one · ⇧A stress (6 distinct users) · X clear.

08Shape language — the system around the smile

The logo is a circle and an arc. Everything in the kit inherits from those two:

1 · Pill — chrome

Ticker, alerts, chips, name tags, the top pills. A pill is a stretched circle: it echoes the face, reads friendly, and never competes with it. Yes — pill is the best container.

2 · Squircle — cards

Content cards use 18–30px continuous radii: soft enough to belong to a circle-first brand, rigid enough to frame captures honestly.

3 · Arc — accents

The smile's curve reused as progress rings (DESK's lesson countdown), underlines, and the ghost watermark on charts. The most literal tie-back we have.

4 · Circle — status

Dots, avatars, LIVE beads. Never triangles or hexagons anywhere — sharp corners fight the face.

09FAQ — the honest answers

Can one HTML file control my camera and window captures?
No — by design. OBS browser sources are sandboxed web pages; OBS itself composites camera/window/display captures. That's why the kit is built as chrome-with-holes: the overlay draws the brand, you place the captures underneath using the cal sheet. Setup is once per scene preset, then it's just scene switching in OBS.
Will people on phones actually be able to read this?
The 1080p canvas letterbox-scales to any window, and since Round 4 there's a hard legibility floor: nothing below 12px, prices ≥ 14px semibold, contrast lifted on all data text. The design is TV-first on purpose — when a phone viewer sees the stream, the numbers survive the downscale.
Viewers spam alerts across YouTube + Twitch + TikTok — will it block the screen?
The stack is capped: max 3 toasts, a 42%-height ceiling with the oldest leaving first, and same-person repeats inside 9s are dropped outright. Run ⇧A (stress test) before going live to watch it behave.
Full-screen segment — I don't want anything on screen.
CINEMA R4 · PURE: one colossal aspect-locked card, the logo-with-writing pill as the sole watermark top-left, everything else off — alerts included until you press A. If you want chrome back without losing the cinematic feel, F flips to BEZEL: pills + ticker inside a rounded device frame, content untouched.
Why pills everywhere — is that really the right shape?
Yes — for containers. Your logo is a circle + arc; the pill is the circle's natural extrusion, so chrome built from pills always matches. Cards use gentler squircles so captures have room, and the arc itself returns as progress rings and the chart watermark. Sharp shapes never enter the kit. Full rationale on the lab index.
